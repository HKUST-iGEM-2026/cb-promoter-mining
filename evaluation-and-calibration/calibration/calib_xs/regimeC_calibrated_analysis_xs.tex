\subsection*{Caveats (read before trusting any number)}

1. \textbf{Genome-scale FP is the metric that matters; balanced AUPRC hides it.} At genome scale promoters
are \textasciitilde{}0.2\% of windows, so a strong balanced classifier still emits many FP peaks per scan. These
tables are the deployment-relevant view; the T22/T25 balanced numbers are not.
2. \textbf{Full vs restricted is one scan, two benchmarks.} The scan is always full-genome; \texttt{restricted}
only adds \texttt{--regions clj\_candidate\_regions.bed} (upstream 300 / into-gene 50 bp windows around
every gene 5'), which shrinks the FP denominator to plausible promoter neighbourhoods. The BED is
\textbf{model-independent} and identical across all models/regimes, which is what keeps the comparison fair.
3. \textbf{Score-only calibrators are a NO-OP on FP counts.} If a model's calibrator chose \texttt{score} only
(\texttt{n\_features\_in\_==1}, see its \texttt{apply\_*.log} below), the transform is monotonic in the score, so
ranking --- and therefore every FP-peak count --- is IDENTICAL to raw. A raw-vs-calibrated difference
for such a model would only be numerical noise. Only score+AT calibrators can move the numbers.
4. \textbf{AT length/domain shift.} The calibrators fit \texttt{at\_fraction} (and its StandardScaler mean/scale)
on the \textasciitilde{}52 bp calibration sequences (93 T9 positives + 186 curated negatives); here AT is over the
81 bp scored window. AT is a fraction (scale-free) so this is the right deployment choice, but the
absolute calibrated probabilities carry that shift. Because the benchmark is rank-based, only the
AT-driven RE-RANKING matters, which is valid regardless of the offset --- so FP counts are affected
far less than absolute probabilities.
5. \textbf{Logit provenance is exact here.} Tracks were scored with \texttt{--emit-logit}, so apply\_calibrator
reads the exact \texttt{logit} (= logit1-logit0) the calibrator was fit on --- no prob$\to$logit inversion,
so the float32-saturation caveat (that only bit the inversion path) does not apply to this run.
6. \textbf{Calibration is prevalence-dependent.} Fit at the 93:186 (1:2) calibration operating point, NOT
genome-wide promoter prevalence (\textasciitilde{}0.2\%). Read calibrated values as recalibrated *scores*, not as
true genome-wide promoter probabilities. The rank-percentile ensemble (T29) is calibration-invariant.
7. \textbf{Truth is incomplete.} The 1,245 TSS are mostly primary / intergenic-sense; deep-CDS and
antisense promoters are under-catalogued. So a counted "false positive" peak may be a real,
unannotated promoter --- treat FP counts as an upper bound on genuine errors.
8. \textbf{genome\_fp\_benchmark settings:} strand-aware, tol 40 bp (a window within 40 bp of a TSS covers
it), FP windows merged into peaks within 81 bp. Contig-aware (chromosome-only here).
9. \textbf{Minor, model-code:} genome\_inference's transformer scorer currently computes softmax after a
\texttt{.float()} cast (the byte-identical prob-ordering refinement was lost in a revert). This can shift
NT's promoter\_score by \textasciitilde{}1e-7 vs the published pre-calibration run; negligible for ranking, but note
it if NT raw numbers differ trivially from the earlier report.
10. \textbf{Held-out genome.} C. ljungdahlii is the held-out in-genus validation genome --- never train or
mine on it (that is T33's circularity rule). It is the Regime-C benchmark precisely because it was
kept out of training.
